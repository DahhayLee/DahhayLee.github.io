Howdy! I'm Dahhay Lee (pronounced DAH-HAY, or just call me Dafne!), and I do research for a living, mostly in biostatistics and survival analysis.

I've spent more than a decade at the National Cancer Center (NCC) in Korea, which I joined after finishing my master's in Applied Statistics at Yonsei University. I came in wanting statistics to make a real difference in the world: to compute numbers that actually matter in people's lives, not vague figures nobody has any use for. I'm also currently a PhD candidate in Computational Science and Engineering at Yonsei. 

Some time after I started at NCC, I came across a tweet that stuck with me. It said, roughly, that the first thing doctors tell you after a cancer diagnosis is: "Don't go online and look up survival rates or any other statistics."

That blew my mind. We statisticians work our butts off to produce those numbers and make them as useful and informative as we can, and the advice patients get is to never look at them? Something has gone seriously wrong. The numbers patients find online are often years out of date, averaged over people who look nothing like them, and presented as if they were certain.

Ever since, my research has centered on one question: how do we make survival analysis reliable, useful, and trustworthy for patients, doctors, and the public? To me, that means two things. Survival statistics have to stay up to date, and their uncertainty has to be addressed honestly.

What I am working on now:

- Bayesian survival models for cancer survival trend analysis
- Uncertainty quantification for survival prediction in cancer
- Drift-aware online updating / Bayesian updating of predictive survival models
- SDE-based survival models

This site will collect my publications, talks, and occasional notes on
methods I find myself explaining more than once.
