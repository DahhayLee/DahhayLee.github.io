Doing things because you can is a pretty decent way to live. Because we can, we climb Everest and cross the Pacific and all that, I suppose... But as a researcher’s attitude, it’s something I’ve gotten sick of. Quitting this kind of research (“I could, so I just threw this method and that method at it”) has been on my mind for the past two years, but it hasn’t been easy. Maybe it’s a chronic affliction of people in applied research. At a recent conference I attended, a lot of researchers gave talks along these lines: “This is something the machine learning people came up with, and nobody in our field had tried it yet, so I gave it a go...”

A precise problem setup, a solution that fits it exactly, and then revising the existing theorems to fix whatever theoretical flaws that solution brings with it: get that far, and you have a decent research paper. The problem is that the world already has a gazillion solutions out there. Honestly, the most efficient path would probably be to take all those solutions other people have built and try them on my own problem... But that doesn’t carry enough research value, so you can’t get it published in a journal, so you have to force yourself to grind out something new, somehow... Those conference talks probably all come from this obsession with newness... This thought has been going around in my head for ten years or so: nothing’s going to get anywhere until we all get over the idea that only something “novel” counts as having academic value.

In all that time nothing has changed. It’s only gotten worse. Conferences are crawling with smart people, but whatever shared sense of mission there used to be is gone, that “we’re doing something really important” feeling, that little bit of exhilaration. The impression I get instead is that everyone’s just absorbed in saying ever more difficult things inside the inner circle, to look like (or be recognized as) someone more impressive. 

Yay, Go team!
